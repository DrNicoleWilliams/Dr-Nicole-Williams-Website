Cognitive Assessments

Cognitive functions include assessment of intellect, memory, phonological skills, academic abilities, attention, executive function, processing speed and visuospatial skills. The aim of the cognitive assessment is to identify a child’s strengths and weaknesses in their learning profiles. Specific weaknesses can affect a child psychologically and educationally in school and so an assessment will help put in place support, adaptations and strategies.

Where appropriate, children may be diagnosed with difficulties such as Dyslexia, Dyscalculia, Dysgraphia or Dyspraxia or specific delays with working memory or information processing, and may be entitled to additional support within the classroom, or special provision for examinations. Decisions about any additional support or examination arrangements are made by the school or the relevant examination board.

What an assessment typically includes

Cognitive abilitiesWechsler Intelligence Scale for Children – Fifth UK Edition (WISC-V UK)
Academic attainmentWechsler Individual Achievement Test – Third UK Edition (WIAT-III UK)
Phonological processingComprehensive Test of Phonological Processing – Second Edition (CTOPP-2)
Visual-motor integrationBeery-Buktenica Developmental Test of Visual-Motor Integration – Sixth Edition (Beery VMI)
Handwriting speedDetailed Assessment of Speed of Handwriting (DASH)
Chosen to fit your childThe measures used are selected according to the referral question and your child's age and individual needs.

What the process looks like

  1. First conversationA free 15-minute call to talk through your concerns and whether an assessment is the right next step.
  2. Questionnaires & school reportsA pre-assessment questionnaire and other screening questionnaires are completed beforehand, and I ask to see recent school reports.
  3. The assessmentCarried out in person, at your home or at your child's school, and lasting approximately 4 hours.
  4. Written reportA comprehensive report with an interpretation of the results, your child's strengths and difficulties, and recommendations to support their learning and development. Usually returned within 2–3 weeks.
  5. Feedback callA call to talk through the report and what to do next.

The report goes to parents or carers, who decide whether to share it with school or anyone else.

Frequently asked questions

Who is a cognitive assessment for?

Children and young people whose parents or carers would like a clearer understanding of how they learn, where there are concerns about progress at school, there are suspected learning difficulties or where they would like to explore their academic potential.

Where do cognitive assessments take place?

In person, either at your home or at your child's school, within a 30-mile radius of Kingston upon Thames, London.

How long does an assessment take, and when will I receive the report?

The assessment lasts approximately 4 hours. The report is usually returned within 2–3 weeks.

How should I explain the assessment to my child?

It helps to keep it simple and positive. For example, you could say that they will be doing different tasks so that we can better understand how they learn, which helps to support their strengths and any areas where a bit more support could be beneficial.

Do you assess for autism or ADHD?

No. My cognitive assessments focus on cognitive abilities, academic attainment and specific learning difficulties, and do not include assessment for autism or ADHD.

Who receives the report?

The report goes to parents or carers, who decide whether to share it with school or anyone else. I don't share it with school myself, although I do ask to see recent school reports as part of the assessment.

How much does a cognitive assessment cost?

Cognitive assessments are £1,200. They can be completed as home visits or at school, within a 30-mile radius of Kingston upon Thames, London.

What is your cancellation policy?

Is everything confidential?

Yes, information is kept confidential, with one important limit: where there are concerns about the safety of your child or of someone else, information will need to be shared with others, for example a GP.

Therapy for Couples & Families

Therapy to help couples and families strengthen relationships, improve communication, navigate conflict and feel more connected.

It’s understandable that relationships can come under strain in times of stress or life transitions. I use a collaborative, inclusive, non-judgemental, trauma-informed, strength-based approach to create a space for supportive conversations and reflections.

As a Clinical Psychologist and Systemic Practitioner (Intermediate level), I support individuals, couples and families, drawing on different perspectives and therapeutic approaches. I bring together an understanding of individual wellbeing with a relational and systemic perspective, exploring how our individual experiences, stories and ways of making sense of the world interact with our relationships and wider contexts. This allows us to develop a fuller understanding of difficulties, recognise patterns and possibilities for change, and find new ways of moving forward.

Together we can explore alternative strategies to support change.

What I can help with

Clinical psychology helps us understand each person’s thoughts, feelings and experiences. Systemic practice looks at the relationships, families and wider contexts around them. I bring the two together, so we can see both what is happening for each of you and how it plays out between you.

Communication & conflictRecurring arguments, feeling unheard, or finding it hard to talk about what matters. We look at what each person is feeling and needing, and at the pattern that plays out between you.
Disconnection & trustGrowing distant, or rebuilding trust. We make sense of each person’s experience and how it shapes the way you connect.
Parenting & family lifeParenting challenges, family conflict and adjusting to new stages, looking at each person’s experience and how the family fits together.
Life transitionsPregnancy and postpartum, separation and divorce, bereavement and other big changes, and how they affect both individual wellbeing and relationships.
Strengthening relationships & improving communicationBuilding on what is already working in your relationship and developing different ways to communicate and connect.
Individual supportAnxiety, stress, trauma and low mood, understood alongside the relationships and wider contexts in your life.

Therapy Sessions

Sessions take place online via MS Teams/Zoom or face-to-face (if applicable) in an agreed location. The typical length of assessment and therapy sessions is 60 minutes, with therapy sessions generally taking place on a weekly basis on the same day and at the same time each week. Sessions run throughout the year apart from dates when the therapist or you are on holiday or unwell.

Therapy sessions can be with individuals, couples or families. If you are having individual sessions, you can be joined at times by significant others (e.g. partners, family or friends) if you so wish and if appropriate.

Further consultation sessions are also available for significant others. The therapist might suggest one or more sessions as helpful in circumstances of a) providing psychoeducation in relation to the type of difficulty that you are experiencing to aid their understanding and to inform ways that they can provide support, b) providing feedback in relation to the work with you, and/or c) forward planning.

Preparing for and planning the ending of the therapeutic work is essential. Please provide the therapist with notice of at least four sessions before the therapy ends, so that she can bring the work to a healthy close to manage the ending rather than an abrupt termination, which can have a negative impact on you.

Cognitive Assessments

The Cognitive Assessment Package is a structured service comprising a pre-assessment call, clinical questionnaires, a main testing assessment session, a final comprehensive written report, and a feedback call.

The main testing assessment session takes place face-to-face and requires an extended appointment block (typically lasting approximately 4 hours).

The final written report will typically be completed and securely emailed to you within 2 to 3 weeks following the completion of the main assessment session, provided all testing has been completed and clinical questionnaires have been returned. Please note that this timeframe may be extended in exceptional circumstances, such as therapist illness or scheduled annual leave, in which case you will be notified.

Under certain circumstances, you have rights under data protection laws in relation to your personal data. Where your child is too young to exercise these rights themselves, a parent or carer with parental responsibility may do so on their behalf.

You have the right to:

Request access to your personal data (commonly known as a "data subject access request"). This enables you to receive a copy of the personal data we hold about you and to check that we are lawfully processing it.

Request correction of the personal data that we hold about you. This enables you to have any incomplete or inaccurate data we hold about you corrected, though we may need to verify the accuracy of the new data you provide to us.

Request erasure of your personal data. This enables you to ask us to delete or remove personal data where there is no good reason for us continuing to process it. You also have the right to ask us to delete or remove your personal data where you have successfully exercised your right to object to processing (see below), where we may have processed your information unlawfully or where we are required to erase your personal data to comply with local law. Note, however, that we may not always be able to comply with your request of erasure for specific legal reasons which will be notified to you, if applicable, at the time of your request.

Object to processing of your personal data where we are relying on a legitimate interest (or those of a third party) and there is something about your particular situation which makes you want to object to processing on this ground as you feel it impacts on your fundamental rights and freedoms. In some cases, we may demonstrate that we have compelling legitimate grounds to process your information which override your rights and freedoms.

Request restriction of processing of your personal data. This enables you to ask us to suspend the processing of your personal data in the following scenarios:

  • If you want us to establish the data's accuracy.
  • Where our use of the data is unlawful but you do not want us to erase it.
  • Where you need us to hold the data even if we no longer require it as you need it to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.
  • You have objected to our use of your data but we need to verify whether we have overriding legitimate grounds to use it.

Request the transfer of your personal data to you or to a third party. We will provide to you, or a third party you have chosen, your personal data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format. Note that this right only applies to automated information which you initially provided consent for us to use or where we used the information to perform a contract with you.

Withdraw consent at any time where we are relying on consent to process your personal data. However, this will not affect the lawfulness of any processing carried out before you withdraw your consent. If you withdraw your consent, we may not be able to provide certain products or services to you. We will advise you if this is the case at the time you withdraw your consent. If you wish to exercise any of the rights set out above, please contact us.

No fee usually required

You will not have to pay a fee to access your personal data (or to exercise any of the other rights). However, we may charge a reasonable fee if your request is clearly unfounded, repetitive or excessive. Alternatively, we could refuse to comply with your request in these circumstances.

What we may need from you

We may need to request specific information from you to help us confirm your identity and ensure your right to access your personal data (or to exercise any of your other rights). This is a security measure to ensure that personal data is not disclosed to any person who has no right to receive it. We may also contact you to ask you for further information in relation to your request to speed up our response.

Time limit to respond

We try to respond to all legitimate requests within one month. Occasionally it could take us longer than a month if your request is particularly complex or you have made a number of requests. In this case, we will notify you and keep you updated.
